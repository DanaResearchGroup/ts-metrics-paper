## 1. Claim clarity

**One-sentence claim:** *Cartesian similarity to a reference TS (RMSD, D-MAE, all variants)
saturates as a predictor of optimization success at an information ceiling AUC ≈ 0.84, because
the outcome is set by the PES not the guess geometry; and reference databases penalize valid,
often lower-energy, alternative saddles.* Made consistently in direction across title
(`:11-12`), abstract (`:28-72`), intro (`:110-127`), results heading (`:236`), discussion
(`:676-723`), conclusion (`:725-749`).

**Numeric drift attached to the claim (all verified against source):**

- **Ceiling quoted two ways for what is framed as one ceiling.** AUC **0.84** at `:60`,
  `:236`, `:302`, `:311`, `:697`, `:728`; AUC **0.83** at `:51`, `:457`, `:488`, `:734`. 0.84
  = best real metric (0.839, `:283`); 0.83 = direction-controlled displacement (0.828/0.822,
  `:452`). The abstract alone quotes both (`:51` and `:60`) for "the ceiling" without saying
  they are two distinct measured quantities. Unify, or state at each site which value is meant.

- **The headline "22%–98%" range is a two-database splice supported by neither.** Quoted at
  `:49`, `:116-117`, `:446`, `:699`, `:731-732` (the most-repeated sentence). Actual
  measurements at `:438-440`: **33%–98% (Transition1x)**, **22%–96% (Reaction-QM)**. The
  headline takes 22% from Reaction-QM and 98% from Transition1x. Fix everywhere to one
  internally sourced range.

- **Lower-energy alternative-saddle fraction: 77% vs 74%.** 77% at `:39-40` and `:364`; **74%**
  at `:684`. Same quantity — reconcile (needs data to know which is right).

## 2. Do the results support the claim?

**The AUC-ceiling conclusion survives the sample-size ambiguity; the derived percentages do
not.** The ceiling is a rank statistic saturating ≈0.77–0.84 across measures (`tab:auc`
`:330-348`; `tab:weights` `:274-290`), corroborated by the independent displacement experiment
at ≈0.83 (`:449-490`). That does not depend on whether n=400 or n=520.

**But the sample size does not reconcile — the most serious internal defect.** Two mutually
exclusive counts are both load-bearing:

- **n=520 / 260 reactions:** abstract `:31`; `tab:weights` caption `:288`; `fig:ceiling`
  caption `:311`; failure decomposition `:361` (239 failures = 46% of 520 ⇒ 54% success,
  matching `:512`, `:408`).
- **n=400 / 200 reactions:** methods `:136` (200 reference TSs); `tab:auc` caption `:249`;
  `:511` ("155 of 400", "2 of 400"). 200 reactions × 2 predictors (`:150-156`) = 400.

`tab:auc` (n=400) and `tab:weights` (n=520) are computed on nominally the same population yet
report different n, so at least one caption is wrong **regardless of the true count**.

**This cannot be resolved on-machine** (it needs the true reaction count). What changes under
each reading:

- **If n=400:** `:136`, `:249`, `:511` are consistent, but the 239-failure decomposition
  `:361` implies 60% failure, contradicting 54% success (`:512`); the §3.2 alternative-saddle
  narrative (`:359-371`) must be recomputed.
- **If n=520:** the decomposition and 54% success are consistent, but `:249`, `:511`, and the
  200-reference count `:136` are wrong.

**Net:** the qualitative claim stands; every count-derived percentage must be recomputed once
n is fixed.

- **Reconciles with neither n:** `:516` says the alternative-saddle share "grows from 19% to
  26%." With 92 alternatives (`:361`) and 73% convergence (`:513`), the T1x share is
  92/(0.73·N) ≈ 31% (n=400) or ≈24% (n=520) — not 19%. Recompute against data.
